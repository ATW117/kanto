# kanto adoption audit (2026-10-16)

> Snapshot as of 2026-10-16, written on the pre-release `audit` branch before the repository was
> public. "This branch" means that branch (now merged into `main`); statements about the repository
> or PyPI returning 404 describe that date.

Question: can a researcher use kanto instead of Pokémon Showdown without caveats that send them
back to Showdown? This audit lists what still stands in the way, checks the parity claims
independently, compares Gen 1 in Showdown master with 0.11.11, surveys which generations recent
papers use, and records the improvements made on the `audit` branch, with measurements.

Machine: NVIDIA DGX Spark (20 aarch64 cores, shared with other jobs; heavy runs used at most 8
threads). Showdown: pokemon-showdown 0.11.11 (npm) and master 7244fe8 (2026-10-16), built
locally. `perf` is not available to unprivileged users here (`perf_event_paranoid = 4`), so
profiles come from a sampling profiler (pprof-rs, SIGPROF).

## 1. Summary

- **Parity holds up.** `parity/reproduce.sh` (new) re-ran every table in docs/SIM_STATUS.md
  with its documented seed: all 22 runs (31,500 state-parity and 8,998 protocol-parity battles)
  gave exactly the documented battle and step counts, with 0 divergences. A second, independent
  route that does not use the harness: kanto battles exported as Showdown input logs replay to
  byte-identical logs in an unmodified Showdown `BattleStream` (3,000 of 3,000 battles).
- **Showdown master changes no Gen 1 battle behaviour since 0.11.11.** A diff of every file Gen 1
  resolves through (gen1 → gen2 → gen3 → gen4 → base mods, `sim/`, PRNG) finds only a Random
  Battle level change (Beedrill 81 → 84) and a ZU tier change. The harness itself was run against
  master: 8,000 fuzzed battles / 570,484 steps, including protocol runs, gave 0 divergences, and
  the 3,000 input-log battles matched.
- **The biggest blockers were interfaces, not mechanics.** kanto did not take Showdown teams,
  had no Showdown server interface, did not ship its team data in a wheel, and could not export
  battles in a form Showdown can replay. All four are now fixed on this branch (section 5).
  poke-env players run unmodified against `python -m kanto.server`.
- **What remains:** (1) Gen 1 only, while most current work is Gen 9; (2) no public repository or
  PyPI release (both URLs return 404), and a broken sdist; (3) Gen 1 formats other than OU;
  (4) a fixed 752-float observation that leaks one bit of hidden information (partial trapping),
  with no structured state access from Python. Section 3 ranks them.
- **Which generation next:** Gen 2 OU, then Gen 3/4 OU. That builds on the Gen 1 work and covers
  Metamon's whole evaluation set. Gen 9 OU is the most-used format, but an exact port is a
  multi-month project (section 4).

Not blockers, checked:
- **Determinism and seeding.** `VecEnv` results do not depend on the thread count (per-env
  derived seeds). `clone_state`/`restore_state` blobs are validated, and int and Showdown seed
  strings both work.
- **Robustness.** Illegal choices and corrupted blobs are rejected without panics. Panics in one
  env are isolated.
- **License.** MIT, with third-party notices. Ship the notices file in the wheel.

## 4. Benchmarks and which generations to add

### What recent work evaluates on

| Work | Venue | Gens / formats | Simulator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PokéAgent Challenge, battling track ([arXiv 2603.15563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15563)) | NeurIPS 2025 competition | **Gen 1 OU and Gen 9 OU** (scored); the server also hosts Gen 2-4 OU and Gen 9 VGC | modified Showdown server |
| Metamon ([arXiv 2504.04395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04395)) | RLC 2025 | **Gen 1-4 OU** (+ UU/NU/Ubers data), later Gen 9 OU | local Showdown (`--no-security`) + poke-env fork |
| PokéChamp ([arXiv 2503.04094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04094)) | ICML 2025 | **Gen 9 OU** (main), Gen 8 Random Battle | Showdown + poke-env |
| PokéLLMon ([arXiv 2402.01118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01118)) | arXiv 2024 | Gen 8 Random Battle | Showdown via poke-env |
| VGC-Bench ([arXiv 2506.10326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10326)) | AAMAS 2026 | Gen 9 VGC (doubles) | Showdown via poke-env |
| Wang, MIT MEng thesis ([DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/153888)) | 2024 | Gen 4 Random Battle | not checked |
| PokéAgent winners: Foul Play (Gen 9 OU), PA-Agent (Gen 1 OU) | 2025 | Gen 9 OU; Gen 1 OU | poke-engine (own Rust engine, not exact); Metamon-based |

Counts over primary evaluations: Gen 9 OU 3 (+1), Gen 1 OU 2 plus every Gen 1 PokéAgent entry,
Gen 2-4 OU 1 (Metamon, and the PokéAgent server), Gen 8 Random Battle 2, Gen 9 VGC 1. None of the
papers names simulator throughput as its bottleneck. They report LLM latency, turn timers and
slow human battles instead. But the strongest search entry wrote its own fast inexact engine, and
offline-RL work fights simulator mismatches, so exactness and speed both matter where search or
self-play is used. (Survey done 2026-10-16 from abstracts and project pages. A 2026 PokéAgent
edition could not be confirmed; the site calls it a living benchmark.)

### Recommendation

1. **Gen 2 OU next.** It is on the PokéAgent server and in Metamon's evaluation set. Gen 2 is
   the generation closest to Gen 1 in the code: it inherits gen3 → gen4 → base like Gen 1, and
   the gen2 mod (scripts 716 lines, moves 802, conditions 257, items 319) is about the size of
   gen1's. A kanto Gen 2 plus the existing poke-env server shim gives Metamon-style researchers
   two of their four generations.
2. **Gen 3-4 OU** after that, for the rest of Metamon's set. They add abilities, natures, EVs
   and more items, but are still small next to Gen 9.
3. **Gen 9 OU / Random Battle** only as a separate project. It covers most of Showdown's base
   `data/` (abilities, items, moves, conditions; tens of thousands of lines) plus
   Terastallization. A Showdown-exact Gen 9 is a re-implementation of most of Showdown.
   pkmn/engine took years to reach Gen 1 and is still working on Gen 2. Alternatives worth
   weighing: a fast exact *replay/validation* layer, or interop (the server shim) with a
   Showdown sim process for Gen 9.

### Effort estimate, same methodology (port the resolved code path, differential harness)

Gen 1 is about 5,100 lines of simulator (kanto-gen1) plus about 3,000 lines of harness, encoder
and client that are partly per-generation.

| Gen | New mechanics vs the previous gen | Simulator size (est.) | Effort (1 experienced engineer) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Special split, held items (about 40 competitive) and berries, weather, Spikes and hazards, Protect/Endure, Baton Pass, Pursuit (switch-out ordering), Curse, Encore, Perish Song, Mean Look, Sleep Talk, Future Sight, Hidden Power, Thief, Belly Drum, Attract, new crit/accuracy rules, 251 species/moves, gender, Gen 2 sleep/freeze rules | 9-11 k lines | **8-12 weeks**: 3-4 port, 2-3 harness/fuzzer (item/weather/hazard scenarios, snapshot mapping), 2-3 protocol + encoder + ClientBattle, 1-2 buffer for divergences |
| 3 | Abilities (76), natures, EVs, IVs, doubles-capable data (singles only), new items, physical/special by type still | +6-8 k | 8-12 weeks |
| 4 | Physical/special split per move, 120+ new moves, items (Choice, Life Orb), abilities | +6-8 k | 8-12 weeks |
| 9 | Everything since: Terastallization, abilities/items/moves of Gens 5-9, field effects, team preview | 40-60 k | 9-18 months |

The harness (PRNG tracing, runner, replay, shrinking, fixtures, mutation testing) is
generation-agnostic apart from the snapshot mapping, so most of the Gen 1 investment carries
over.

### Gen 2 design sketch

- `crates/kanto-gen2`: a `Copy` battle state like Gen 1's. `Mon` gains item, gender, happiness,
  a separate SpA/SpD, and a Hidden Power type and power derived from DVs. `Side` gains Spikes,
  Safeguard, Reflect and Light Screen as side conditions (Gen 2 moves them off the Pokémon), and
  the future-sight slot. `Battle` gains weather and its turn counter. Target about 1 KB.
- Port the resolved code of `Dex.mod('gen2')`: gen2's `scripts.ts` (`runMove`, `useMove`,
  `getDamage` with items and weather, `tryMoveHit`), then gen3's and gen4's overrides, then base.
  As in Gen 1, follow the inherited path explicitly and record each origin (docs/SIM_DESIGN.md
  style).
- Event ordering: Gen 2 has many more simultaneous handlers (items, weather, residual order:
  Leftovers, Future Sight, weather, Spikes on switch-in), so the "build the present handlers in
  Showdown's discovery order + `speed_sort`" machinery needs a table of (event, effect) handler
  keys per Gen 2 effect. Generate it from Showdown's data where possible
  (`tools/gen_sim_data.js` already resolves the dex).
- `kanto-data/src/gen2.rs` from `tools/gen_sim_data.js` pointed at `Dex.mod('gen2')`: species,
  moves, items, type chart, learnsets.
- Harness: a `snapshot.rs` mapping for Gen 2 state, and runner.js generalised by format id. Fuzzer
  team kinds: random, legal, Gen 2 OU usage teams, and targeted scenarios (Pursuit vs switch,
  Baton Pass chains, Perish Song + trap, Spikes + Rapid Spin, Encore + choice lock, berries at
  thresholds, weather + Thunder/Solar Beam, Future Sight + switch, Sleep Talk, Curse Ghost/
  non-Ghost). Gate releases on mutation testing as Gen 1 does.
- Env: a Gen 2 `Backend` and a new observation layout (items, weather, side conditions), as
  `OBS_VERSION` 2-gen2. `ClientBattle` reconstruction is more work: item reveals, and Leftovers
  and berry inference.
- Ship order: battle state + damage formula + PRNG parity on a random-move fuzzer first, then
  items and weather, then the protocol and request JSON, then the env.

Profiling notes (for whoever continues the performance work):
- **Engine** (single thread, gen1ou pool, uniform random): 5.07 M turns/s (vanilla) vs
  pkmn/engine 6.81 M on the same games. That is 113 ns per update vs 72, and choices take 25 ns
  vs 13. Sampled profiles put about 85% in `apply_choices` (`run_move` dominates) and about 14%
  in `legal_choices`. The code is already branch-light and allocation-free. Build settings (fat
  LTO, `target-cpu=native`, codegen-units) were measured by the authors as noise. PGO could not
  be tried: the system's llvm-profdata is LLVM 18, rustc's is 23, and installing `llvm-tools`
  would change the shared toolchain.
- **Env**: before this change, `VecEnv.step` spent about 44% in `obs::encode`, about 20% in
  `view` (done twice per env) and only about 15% in the simulator. At N = 4096 the 24 MB
  observation buffer does not fit in cache (N = 256: 744 ns per env-step; N = 4096: 919 ns). A
  further 15-25% looks available from writing the observation in one pass, without `fill(0)`
  followed by sparse writes, and from an f16/u8 observation option. Two micro-optimisations were
  tried and dropped as unmeasurable: integer `assumed_stats`, and fixed-size output arrays.

## 7. Marketing check (README as a skeptical reader)

Overstated or unsupported, now corrected in README.md:
1. "roughly 1,000 times faster than Showdown itself." The two figures measure different things:
   kanto's engine figure builds no protocol text and Showdown's always builds its log. The
   README now gives both numbers with that context. A fair "kanto with protocol text vs
   Showdown" engine figure was not measured; the env-level figure (Python-driven `ProtocolBattle`,
   about 1,250 games/s) is not comparable either.
2. "The 752-float observation holds only what a Showdown player can see." False for partial
   trapping, per docs/CLIENT.md. The exception is now stated.
3. "At 16 threads ... about 2.5 M env-steps/s." Re-measured at 1 and 8 threads after this
   branch's change (1.0 M and 2.8-3.1 M); 16 threads were not used on the shared machine.
4. "No server" and "Parity is with 0.11.11 ... later releases may change Gen 1 behaviour" were
   accurate but out of date. Now: `kanto.server`, and master checked on 2026-10-16.
5. "Teams are checked for Gen 1 legality." True, but the README did not say that tier bans were
   not checked. Now documented, with `validate_team`.

Missing evidence, now added:
- Reproduction instructions for the exact parity tables: `parity/reproduce.sh` with the
  documented seeds, re-run with identical counts.
- An independent check that does not trust the harness: input-log replay through stock
  Showdown.
- An explicit "fuzzing is evidence, not proof" sentence.

Still worth adding (not done):
- The install instructions point at a repository that is not public.
- No archived release or DOI. The citation block says so itself.
- The mutation-testing survivors are "argued equivalent". A short proof sketch per survivor in
  SIM_STATUS exists, which is reasonable, but an independent reviewer has not checked them.
- Throughput figures come from one machine (DGX Spark, aarch64). An x86_64 number would help
  readers compare with pkmn/engine's published figures.
- Coverage numbers (moves used in matched steps) come from uniform-random play. Battles between
  strong players reach different states (long stall endgames, specific PP situations). Fuzzing
  with the shipped usage-based pool or real ladder teams and heuristic policies, for example
  `max_damage` vs `lookahead`, would strengthen the claim for realistic play.

## 8. Reproducing this report

```bash
export PS_DIR=~/ps/node_modules/pokemon-showdown           # npm install pokemon-showdown@0.11.11
parity/reproduce.sh all 5                                  # parity tables, documented seeds
cargo run --release -p kanto-parity --bin parity -- teams --random 10000 --level100
pytest tests/python/test_showdown_interop.py               # input logs through BattleStream (120 battles)
pip install poke-env websockets && pytest tests/python/test_server.py
python bench/bench.py --env-only --envs 4096 --threads 1,8
cargo run --release -p kanto-backend --features pkmn --example bench -- 20000 data/teams/gen1ou
```

For Showdown master, clone smogon/pokemon-showdown, run `npm install` and `node build`, then
point `PS_DIR` at the checkout.
